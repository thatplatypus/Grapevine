How a repeat is found, and what it is worth

A compressor of this kind makes text smaller in one way only: where bytes come again
that came before, it does not write them a second time. It writes how many there are and
how far back they were, and whoever reads the stream copies them from what was already
read. A word that was used a page ago costs a few bits the second time. A word that was
never used costs every one of its letters.

So the work is to find, for the bytes at hand, the longest stretch that came before.
Looking through everything that came before would take too long, and the format allows a
repeat to reach back 32,768 bytes and no further. So a table is kept of where each run of
three bytes was last seen, and each place remembers the place before it that began with
the same three bytes. A search starts at the nearest such place and walks back from one
to the next. At each it counts how many bytes are the same, and it keeps the longest it
has found.

Three things bound the search. A repeat is never longer than 258 bytes, because that is
the longest the format can say. It is never further back than the window allows. And the
walk looks at a few places and then stops, however many there are. Without that last
bound, input in which the same three bytes come again and again would send every search
down a chain of thousands of places, and a megabyte would take minutes. With it, the
worst input costs about what ordinary text costs.

What a repeat is worth depends on how it is written. Here the codes are the fixed ones
that the format gives for nothing: a byte that repeats nothing costs eight or nine bits,
and a repeat costs twelve to twenty-five, whatever it stands for. So a repeat of three
bytes is hardly a saving, and a repeat of thirty is most of one. A compressor that worked
out its own codes for each input would give the common letters shorter codes and do
better on text by a quarter or so. That is left for later. The repeats are where most of
the saving is, and they are what this fixture is here to measure: if its stream grows,
the search has begun to find fewer of them, or shorter ones, than it did.
